About

I am a PhD student in the James Watt School of Engineering at the University of Glasgow, supervised by Prof. Lei Zhang.

My research is on trustworthy multi-agent LLM systems, centred on selective commitment: when an LLM system, or a group of agents, should act on its evidence, and when it should abstain or flag a conflict instead. I am particularly interested in failures where systems over-commit under partial or conflicting evidence, and in evaluation methods that separate task success from evidence-supported decisions.

Before moving to AI, I trained in communication engineering, and I still draw on ideas from distributed systems and Byzantine fault tolerance in my work.

Research agenda

I am currently working on three related questions:

  1. When should an LLM system commit to a verdict? Measuring when LLM judges and agents give directional answers on mixed evidence that warrants a non-directional verdict.
  2. How does distributed evidence become action? Studying how evidence spread across agent roles is reported, combined and turned into decisions, and when the resulting actions are actually supported by that evidence.
  3. When can agreement among agents be certified? Designing admissibility checks that let a group of possibly faulty agents commit to a shared semantic outcome, or refuse to commit when agreement is insufficient.
