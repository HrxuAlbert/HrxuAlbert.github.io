Current research

Ongoing projects

Evidence-aware decision making

When do model judgements go beyond what the evidence supports? I study unsupported commitment, conflicting evidence, and when rejection or abstention is warranted.

Multi-agent evidence communication

How is distributed evidence selected, summarised, communicated, and integrated? I examine information loss, distortion, and unsupported conclusions across agent reports and decisions.

LLM and agent evaluation

How can we distinguish well-supported decisions from lucky outcomes? I build evaluations that expose evidence gaps and help people decide when human review is needed.

I am moving towards reinforcement learning and post-training to improve how models act on conflicting or incomplete evidence, using these evaluations as training targets and checks.

Background

About me

I am a PhD student in the James Watt School of Engineering at the University of Glasgow, supervised by Prof. Lei Zhang and Prof. Iadh Ounis.

I study how LLMs and multi-agent LLM systems use evidence to reach decisions. Much of my work looks at over-commitment: LLM judges and agents giving definitive answers when the evidence is partial or conflicting, and agent pipelines that succeed at a task without the evidence to support the action they took.

I build evaluations that make these failures visible, tracing evidence through agent reports, decisions and outcomes so that people can tell a well-supported decision from a lucky one, and know when human review is needed.

Beyond diagnosing these failures, I am moving towards fixing them: using reinforcement learning and post-training to teach models to keep their decisions within what the evidence supports, with the evaluations above serving as targets and checks for that training.
