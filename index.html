About

I am a PhD student in the James Watt School of Engineering at the University of Glasgow, supervised by Prof. Lei Zhang and Prof. Iadh Ounis.

I study how LLMs and multi-agent LLM systems use evidence to reach decisions. Much of my work looks at over-commitment: LLM judges and agents giving definitive answers when the evidence is partial or conflicting, and agent pipelines that succeed at a task without the evidence to support the action they took.

I build evaluations that make these failures visible, tracing evidence through agent reports, decisions and outcomes so that people can tell a well-supported decision from a lucky one, and know when human review is needed.

Research agenda

I am currently working on three related questions:

  1. When do LLM judgements go beyond the evidence? Measuring when LLM judges and agents issue definitive verdicts on evidence that is conflicting or insufficient, and separating whether they recognise the conflict from what they finally decide.
  2. How does evidence flow through multi-agent decisions? Tracing evidence through agent reports, decisions and task outcomes to locate information loss and unsupported claims, and to distinguish success supported by the available information from success achieved without it.
  3. How can evaluation support human oversight? Designing evaluations that help people recognise uncertainty, question unsupported conclusions and decide when an automated judgement needs human review.
